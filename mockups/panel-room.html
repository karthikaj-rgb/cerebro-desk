Option APanel folds to a rail

When a room opens, the panel slides down to a thin rail with the creature on it. The room gets the full width and behaves exactly like Bottom mode: words under the creature, choices under the data, one place to look. Close the room and the panel unfolds, with the whole exchange already in its thread.

  • Nothing to learn: the room is the room you already know.
  • Panel becomes history and the place to start things, not the place to act.
  • Loses the "AI is always on the left" feeling while you're in a room.

Option B · quickestChoices follow the eye

Keep the panel, but while a room is open it only logs. The AI's line and the choices sit in the room, under the data, where you're already looking. The panel's thread dims to show it's history, and its box still works if you'd rather type.

  • One-line change to what's there now.
  • The panel keeps the record; the room keeps the action.
  • Still two surfaces on screen, so it's a compromise, not a resolution.

Option C · recommendedNo room: the card opens in place

In panel mode a click never opens an overlay. The card you clicked grows in place and shows what the AI is working from; the other cards dim. The AI speaks in the panel, and its choices sit right under its latest line, with a thin line tying the line to the open card. Rooms only open for live work and builds, where a stage is worth it.

  • One conversation, one place to act, and the desk never disappears.
  • Matches the Option C promise: the panel is the AI.
  • Biggest build: card "open in place" states for every card type, plus Scribble.
My pick: C. If you want it today, B first, then C.
