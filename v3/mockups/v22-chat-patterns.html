Chats · one experience, used where it helps

The plan card is the right pattern whenever the AI is about to do something. It’s the wrong pattern for answers, memory, and building with the canvas. Below: where to use it, and three options to make the whole flow lighter, using Harbor as the example.

Where the plan card belongs

Rule of thumb: if the AI will change something, show a plan card. If it’s only telling you something, answer in text (with a small visual). If it’s building something you need to see, use the canvas.

Three options for the flow itself

Today a fix makes six blocks: the AI line, the plan card, your “Switch card” bubble, “Did 4 steps”, “Done. Harbor’s ads are live again.”, and the result line. Same information, too many pieces.

AThe card becomes the receipt

One card for the whole life of the job: it asks, then works inside itself (steps tick in place with a thin progress bar), then turns into the result with Undo, Repeat and Why in its footer. No echo bubble, no separate steps row, no “Done.” line.

The plan card stays as the ask. Once you approve, it shrinks to one line and everything after is a single receipt line you can open for the steps. Calm history: scroll back and each job is one row.

My pick: A · The card becomes the receipt for fixes, tasks, campaigns and channel jobs. It cuts six blocks to one, and the agent’s face carries through ask → working → done, which makes the AI feel like it’s doing the work in front of you. Follow-ups as mini cards, answers as text + small visual, building on the canvas, memory as the remember row.
